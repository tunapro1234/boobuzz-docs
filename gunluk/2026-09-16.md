### Later that day — R3/R4/R5 completion and simulator hardening

The remainder of the day moved from design to implementation. R3 completed the
`RequestBatch` seam, direct map, turret contract, `cplx1` logic modules, teleop
map, and loop-boundary engine switch (`09f7d71` through `d4226eb`). R4 then
published three asynchronous seam lines, deterministic bags and replay, the
feedback socket controller, and the tap/drive tools (`c80e4ff` through
`33ace3f`).

The robot review follow-up closed the named safety paths: reset ownership, RPM
consistency, stale handoff output, turret and shooter cancellation, manual
sequence takeover, attached-request barriers, switch-status forwarding, status
cleanup, path validation, Android network permission, bounded tap/socket queues,
socket watchdog cancellation, strict batch validation, shutdown joins, minSdk
compatibility, replay pose precedence, seeded bit-equal replay, intake ownership,
and synchronous bag-path validation. The review record still calls out the
remaining real-HAL and broader integration-test work rather than claiming it is
complete.

S3 added shared-world multi-robot contact/determinism, viewer robot selection,
and a two-robot runner (`6dfabff`, `238e91d`, `76db5f4`); sim-cx-13 added the
`back` and `start` gamepad fields (`b083b92`). No sim-cx-14 commit appears in the
history. Later simulator work added protocol validation, lockstep deadlines and
reset epochs, strict three-seam bag groups, tap-drop metadata, numeric event and
servo validation, tap reconnect, and a demo launcher.

Docs recorded the S3/R4 design revision (`d1d009c`), protocol field update
(`8512985`), R3/R4/simulator reviews (`d14bf3d`, `77ba16a`, `f207977`), the R5
addendum (`ca8632f`), and the simulator response (`9a0babd`). The architecture
was then rendered as a diagram-first 18-page PDF with 23 diagrams (`fed3467`).
